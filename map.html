    WATER QUALITY

    What the numbers mean

    Each station's severity score combines five common water-quality indicators. The ranges below come from the ten simulated stations in this demo dataset.

    pH acidity / alkalinity

    Neutral water is close to pH 7. Strongly acidic or alkaline water can harm fish and plants and may point to chemical discharge.

    Score weight: 12% · distance from pH 7

    DO dissolved oxygen

    Fish and other aquatic life need oxygen dissolved in water. Low DO often means organic waste is being broken down.

    Score weight: 25% · lower is worse

    BOD₅ biochemical oxygen demand

    How much oxygen microbes use over five days to break down organic matter. High values suggest sewage or organic wastewater.

    Score weight: 24% · higher is worse

    COD chemical oxygen demand

    Oxygen needed to chemically break down pollutants, including substances microbes cannot digest, such as some industrial chemicals.

    Score weight: 22% · higher is worse

    TSS total suspended solids · turbidity

    Particles floating in the water that make it cloudy. This dataset has no separate turbidity reading, so TSS is used as the closest indicator.

    Score weight: 17% · higher is worse

    Heavy metals lead, mercury, cadmium…

    Metals from some industrial processes can build up in fish and sediment. They need laboratory testing.

    Not measured in demo dataset. Not included in the score.

    How the severity score is calculated

    Each reading is turned into a 0–100 sub-score, then the sub-scores are weighted and added:

    pH  sub-score = |pH − 7| ÷ 2.5 × 100
DO  sub-score = (8 − DO) ÷ 7.5 × 100
BOD sub-score = BOD₅ ÷ 60 × 100
COD sub-score = COD ÷ 160 × 100
TSS sub-score = TSS ÷ 180 × 100   (each limited to 0–100)

Score = 0.12·pH + 0.25·DO + 0.24·BOD + 0.22·COD + 0.17·TSS

    This formula was designed by the EcoGuard team for learning purposes. It is not an official water-quality index, and the station values are simulated.
